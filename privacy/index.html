Privacy Policy

Last updated November 20, 2026

In short

  • Pouf has no accounts, no servers, no analytics and no ads.
  • The iPhone app and the Mac helper only talk to each other, encrypted, on your local network.
  • We don't collect, sell or share any personal data.

What this policy covers

This policy covers the Pouf app for iPhone, the Pouf Helper app for Mac and this website. "We" means the developer of Pouf.

What Pouf doesn't do

  • There is no account and no sign-in.
  • There is no analytics, advertising, tracking or crash-reporting code in either app.
  • Neither app connects to the internet. They don't contact our servers or anyone else's, and we don't run any servers.

What stays on your devices

On your iPhone

  • The pairing key for your Mac, stored in the Keychain and limited to this iPhone.
  • Your favorite apps, your app groups and your settings, such as pointer speed, natural scrolling and the Face ID lock.

On your Mac

  • The name and pairing key of each iPhone you pair, stored in the Keychain.
  • The helper's settings, such as Listening and Launch at Login.

This information stays on your devices. Like other app data, it can be part of backups you make yourself, such as iCloud Backup or Time Machine. Both apps also write short diagnostic messages to the system log on the device, for example when a connection closes. These stay on the device too.

What travels between your iPhone and your Mac

While your iPhone is connected to your Mac, the two exchange:

  • From the iPhone: pointer movement, clicks and scrolling, the keys you press and the text you type, media and app commands, and the iPhone's name when it connects.
  • From the Mac: the Mac's name, the apps installed and running on it, their icons, and which app is in front.

This goes directly between your two devices on your local network, never through a server. It is encrypted with TLS 1.2 for commands and DTLS 1.2 for pointer movement, using a key that only your paired devices hold. Neither app stores the text you type or the commands you send.

So your iPhone can find it, the Mac announces itself on your local network under a generic name such as Pouf-a1b2c3, not its real name. You can stop this at any time with the Listening switch in the Pouf menu.

Permissions

  • Local Network (iPhone and Mac): to find each other and connect.
  • Camera (iPhone): only to scan the pairing code. Camera images are processed on the iPhone and are not saved or sent anywhere.
  • Face ID (iPhone): to lock the app. iOS handles Face ID; Pouf only learns whether it succeeded and never has access to your face data.
  • Accessibility (Mac): to move the pointer, click and press keys for you.
  • Notifications (Mac): to tell you when a new iPhone pairs.

You can turn any of these off in Settings on the iPhone or System Settings on the Mac. The features that need them then stop working.

Deleting your data

  • iPhone: Unpair This Mac in Pouf's settings deletes the pairing key. Deleting the app removes its favorites, groups and settings.
  • Mac: removing an iPhone under Devices in the Pouf menu deletes its pairing key. To remove the helper, quit it and move it from Applications to the Trash.

This website and downloads

This website is hosted on GitHub Pages, and Pouf Helper is downloaded from GitHub. GitHub may record technical data such as your IP address when you visit or download; see the GitHub General Privacy Statement. The site itself sets no cookies, uses no analytics and loads nothing from other websites.

Email

If you email us, we use your address and what you write only to reply to you. We don't add you to any list or share your email with anyone.

Children

Pouf doesn't collect personal data from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date.
